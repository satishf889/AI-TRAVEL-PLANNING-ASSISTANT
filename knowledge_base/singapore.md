# Singapore

## Overview
Singapore (Chinese: 新加坡 Xīnjiāpō; Malay: Singapura; Tamil: சிங்கப்பூர் Chingappur) is a city-state in Southeast Asia. Modern Singapore was founded as a British trading colony in 1819 and, since independence, has become one of the world's most prosperous countries and home to one of the world's busiest ports. The country has around six million people and is one of the world's most densely populated countries (second only to Monaco). Singapore is known as a "Garden City"—over 50% of its area is covered in greenery, with over 50 major parks and 4 nature reserves.

Singapore combines skyscrapers and shopping malls with a medley of Asian cultures and a tropical climate. Singaporean food is notable for bustling hawker centres and 24-hour coffee shops offering affordable food from across Asia. The city is sometimes referred to as the Little Red Dot or the Lion City (Singapura, Sanskrit for "Lion City"). It also has a partly deserved reputation for sterile predictability and is occasionally called the "Switzerland of Asia."

## Districts

### Central Business District (CBD)
- Located in the south of the island (coordinates given in source: 1°17′30″N 103°51′1″E).
- Riverside / Civic District: Singapore's colonial core with museums, statues and theatres; restaurants, bars and clubs centred along the Singapore River at Boat Quay and Clarke Quay.
- Orchard Road: Miles of shopping malls in air-conditioned comfort. The eastern end includes the Bras Basah District, an arts and culture project in progress.
- Marina Bay: Dominated by Marina Bay Sands (hotel, casino, shopping mall, convention centre and museum), Gardens by the Bay, and the Marina Barrage. The Singapore Flyer and the Esplanade make up the new iconic skyline.
- Bugis and Kampong Glam: Singapore's old Malay district, good for daytime shopping and comes to life at night.
- Chinatown: Designated for Chinese settlement by Raffles; a Chinese heritage area popular with tourists. Restored shophouses are trendy hangouts.
- Little India: A piece of India to the north of the city core.

### Outer Singapore
- Sentosa and Harbourfront: A separate island once a military fort, developed into a resort with attractions including gambling and Universal Studios. Across the water are Mount Faber and the Southern Ridges (an urban treetop walk). The Southern Islands are covered as part of this area; some are accessible to tourists by ferry.
- East Coast: A largely residential eastern part of the island that contains Changi Airport, extensive beaches and many famous eateries. Also covers Geylang Serai (the home of Singapore's Malays) and Pulau Ubin, a rustic remnant of old Singapore.
- North and West: The northern and western parts (Woodlands and Jurong) are residential and industrial hinterlands. A major tourist attraction is the Mandai complex, containing all four of Singapore's zoos.
- Balestier, Newton, Novena and Toa Payoh: Areas offering budget accommodation and Burmese temples within easy reach of Central Singapore. Toa Payoh is one of Singapore's first planned neighbourhoods and offers a way to experience local housing estate design.

## Addresses
- Central addressing is similar to other English-speaking countries (e.g., "17 Orchard Rd").
- Public housing addresses often look like: "Blk 505 Bedok Nth Ave 3 #19-315":
  - "Blk 505" = block number.
  - "Bedok Nth Ave 3" = street name and number.
  - "#19-315" = floor 19, apartment 315.
- The first digit of both the housing block and street number indicates the neighbourhood number (example given: both starting with 5).
- Singapore uses 6-digit postal codes; the last three digits correspond to exactly one building. Example: "Blk 181 Bedok Nth Rd" corresponds to postal code "S'pore 460181".
- Common Malay address terms: Jalan (Jln) = Road, Lorong (Lor) = Lane, Bukit (Bt) = Hill, Kampong (Kg) = Village.
- Useful online tools: StreetDirectory.com, GoThere.sg and OneMap.sg. When searching, omit "Blk" and the unit number (e.g., enter "505 Bedok Nth Ave 3").

## Understand
- Singapore is a microcosm of Asia, populated by Chinese, Malays and Indians, along with a large group of workers and expatriates from around the globe.
- The country is small enough to be crossed in barely an hour.
- Singapore celebrated its 50th birthday in 2015.
- Economic pragmatism and redevelopment are prominent (examples: Marina Bay Sands, Resorts World Sentosa), but there are also efforts to preserve local heritage in areas such as Balestier.

## Local Experiences
- Food: Singaporean food culture centers on hawker centres and 24-hour coffee shops offering affordable and diverse Asian cuisine.
- Parks and greenery: The city emphasizes green space and parks, earning the "Garden City" moniker.
- Shopping and nightlife: The city is known for good shopping and a vibrant nightlife scene, with large shopping malls and late-night areas.

## History
- Earliest mentions of Singapore appear in 2nd–3rd century Greek and Chinese texts.
- Legend: In the 13th century, Srivijayan prince Sang Nila Utama landed on the island, saw an animal he thought was a lion, and founded Singapura ("Lion City"). There were never any lions in the region; the animal was more likely a tiger or wild boar.
- The island was an important port for various kingdoms but was destroyed by Portuguese raiders in 1613 and faded into obscurity.
- Modern history began in 1819 when Sir Thomas Stamford Raffles made a deal with a claimant to the Sultanate of Johor throne to establish a British trading post on the island.
- The Dutch initially protested British presence. An Anglo-Dutch treaty in 1824 separated the Malay world into British and Dutch spheres of influence, contributing to the present-day Malaysia–Indonesia and Singapore–Indonesia borders.
